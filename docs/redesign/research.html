What people love in money apps, and what FiNN should take from it

A study of 16 finance apps, design-community work and user reviews, written to decide FiNN's new look before any code changes.

How this was gathered. Web search across store trackers, reviews, vendor posts and design write-ups; five competitor marketing sites opened in Chrome to read their live fonts, colours and button styles; every candidate typeface downloaded and checked for the ₹ glyph. Store pages, Mobbin, Page Flows and Screenlane were not browsed (login walls, and no page-fetch tool this session), so star ratings come from third-party trackers and are quoted with their source. Most 2026 review coverage is affiliate content; claims that rest on one weak source are marked single source. Motion notes describe public product behaviour and marketing footage, not teardown of the apps.

The apps at a glance

Fi Money was dropped from the study: it wound down its banking service in 2025. Mint closed in 2024; its users mostly moved to Monarch, Copilot and Credit Karma.

Design community: what shots show, and what survives contact with real data

  • Dribbble expense-tracker shots (for example Expense Tracker App, Finance tracker case study, Finance Management, Wallet App) cluster around gradient hero cards, 3D credit cards, glass panels, oversized donuts and short round numbers.
  • Real Indian figures break those layouts: ₹12,34,567.89 is 12 characters with Indian grouping, and category names like "EMI & Loan Repayment" overflow chips designed for "Food".
  • Case studies that tested with users report the same fixes: simpler onboarding, designed empty states and more visible budget alerts (finio, an app for young Indian professionals, tested with 8 people); calm, reduced feature sets for anxious first budgeters (BDGT on Behance).
  • Pattern-library analysis of 1,460 onboarding flows found finance onboarding is among the longest and works when it reaches the "aha" moment quickly; few apps personalise it.

Patterns that line up with high satisfaction

  1. One honest headline number, explained on tap. PocketGuard's "In My Pocket", YNAB's "Ready to Assign", Copilot's "left of budgeted". FiNN already has the best version of this ("Available to spend" = cash minus set aside).
  2. Logging in two or three taps. Axio removes typing; YNAB lost a star average by adding taps. Amount first, category second, save.
  3. Interactive, calm charts. Copilot's scrub-able cash flow and month-over-month lines beat static donuts. One chart per question.
  4. A home you can arrange. Monarch widgets, Revolut 10. Ranked by what the person uses, not by feature count.
  5. Shared money treated as first class. Monarch's free couples sharing, Splitwise's loyalty despite its UI.
  6. A non-judgemental voice. Fold shows commitments without telling you to cancel; Cleo's roast is opt-in. Spending is normal; only going over a limit you set is a warning.
  7. Nothing between a person and the task. No ads, upsells or long reward animations in paid or core flows (YNAB, Emma, Splitwise, CRED critiques).
  8. Accuracy over beauty. Copilot's top complaints are wrong or late transactions. Visible system states (saved, pending, failed) build trust.

Standout animations and components, specified to rebuild

Specs are FiNN's own interpretation of the pattern, not a copy of any app's implementation.

Rolling figure counter

Hero amounts change digit by digit, each digit on its own vertical track, 420 ms, spring (stiffness 380, damping ratio 0.86), rightmost digit settles first, tabular figures so width never jumps. Count up from the previous value, never from zero after the first load. Reduced motion: swap instantly. Seen in Revolut's balance header and Copilot's totals.

Budget ring and bar fill

Ring starts at 12 o'clock and fills clockwise to spent / limit, 600 ms ease-out cubic, stroke caps rounded; colour changes only at 85% (amber) and 100% (red), never as a gradient. A tick on the track marks where spending should be today (FiNN's pace marker). Reference: Copilot budget rings.

Scrub-able spend line

Cumulative spend this month against last month (dashed) and the budget pace. Long-press (300 ms) shows a vertical cursor; the value pins above the chart, not under the finger. Haptic selection tick only when the cursor crosses a day boundary, at most one per 60 ms, because dense ticks feel disconnected (user report). Reference implementation: Coinbase CDS Scrubber; pattern from Copilot and Rocket Money.

Chart draw-in

Lines draw left to right by path length, 700 ms ease-out; bars grow from the baseline with a 24 ms stagger, capped at 8 bars. Runs once per data change, never on every rebuild. Reduced motion: fade 120 ms.

Cash-flow ribbon (mobile Sankey)

Monarch's Sankey made compact: one horizontal ribbon, income width = 100%, split into set aside, spent by top 4 categories and free. Tap a segment to expand it into rows. Reference: Monarch cash-flow Sankey.

Amount-first keypad sheet

Add expense opens as a bottom sheet already focused on a large amount display with a custom keypad (no system keyboard jump). Category chips (recent first) sit above the keypad; "Save" stays reachable by the thumb. Sheet uses a spring (stiffness 300, damping ratio 0.9) with a 25% and 92% snap. Each key: 60 ms scale to 0.94 and a light haptic.

Plunk button (pressable depth)

CRED's open-source NeoPOP button shows a hard edge that collapses as you press. FiNN version: 3 px bottom edge in a darker tone, press translates the face 3 px down in 70 ms, release springs back. Reserve for the one primary action per screen. Reference: NeoPOP.

Row to detail shared element

Tapping a transaction row morphs its category tile and amount into the detail header (Flutter Hero, 320 ms, emphasised easing). Keeps place and makes the detail feel like the same object.

Swipe actions on rows

Swipe left reveals Edit and Delete; past 60% width the delete commits with a medium haptic and an Undo toast for 5 s. Swipe right on a pending row marks it settled. Always mirrored by a long-press menu for screen readers.

Skeleton, pull-to-refresh, success

Skeletons match final layout (no shimmer sweep wider than the card; 1.4 s cycle). Pull-to-refresh uses the brand mark filling as you pull. Success is a 280 ms check stroke plus a soft haptic; a celebration (a short burst of brand shapes) only for a goal reached or a debt fully settled.

Anti-patterns to avoid

  • Adding taps or hiding fields in the add-transaction flow.
  • Ads, upsells or "rate us" prompts inside a task.
  • Reward animations longer than 400 ms between a person and the next step.
  • Red for all spending. Red means over a limit you set; ordinary spending stays ink.
  • Deadlines, "overdue" or "late" language for informal debts between people (FiNN rule).
  • Donuts with more than five slices, legends far from the data, colour as the only signal.
  • Grey-on-grey labels under 13 px, glass panels over busy backgrounds, pure black dark mode.
  • Proportional figures in money columns, and zeros that read as Ø (FiNN's current font).
  • A home that lists every module; clutter is the top complaint in Indian super-apps.

What this means for FiNN

  • Keep the substance: available vs set aside, budget pace and run-out dates, debt aging, exact EMI math. These are rarer and more honest than most competitors.
  • Fix the shell: one theme source of truth (today the Material theme and the screens read two different colour sets), a nav bar that does not collide with the add button, and a number face built for money.
  • Make logging the fastest thing in the app and the assistant the most characterful.
  • Spend motion on three moments only: the hero number, chart draw-in and the save confirmation.
